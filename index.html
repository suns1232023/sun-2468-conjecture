The Conjecture

The Sun's (2,4,6,8) Binomial Representation Conjecture concerns representations of integers in the form

\[ n = \binom{w}{2} + \binom{x}{4} + \binom{y}{6} + \binom{z}{8} \] \[ w \ge 2,\qquad x \ge 4,\qquad y \ge 6,\qquad z \ge 8. \]

The research program investigates whether every positive integer admits such a representation, together with the structural mechanisms governing the corresponding additive sumsets.

Abstract

We study Sun's (2,4,6,8) binomial representation conjecture and develop a witness-geometric reformulation through global crossing transfer and defect-capacity analysis.

The principal contributions of Version V15.6 include:

  • The exact identity \[ w^*(n)=K(n)+\Delta(n)+2. \]
  • Identification of the extremal transfer constant \[ C_T=\frac{64}{19}. \]
  • Verification of the subcritical constant \[ C_K= \frac{118202}{59375} <2. \]
  • Exhaustive computational verification up to \[ N=2,000,000. \]
  • Reduction of the remaining difficulty to an analytic closure problem concerning short-interval coverage of \[ H=S_4+S_6+S_8. \]

Research Structure

Witness Geometry

The framework studies the geometry of admissible witnesses and the way local representation capacity propagates through the additive structure of the binomial sumsets.

Global Crossing Transfer

The transfer framework converts information about local gaps and witness capacity into global coverage statements for the relevant Minkowski sumsets.

Analytic Closure

The remaining open component concerns the analytic control required to convert the established finite and combinatorial structure into a complete global coverage theorem.
