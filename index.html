Epistemic Classification Notice

All mathematical statements in this work strictly adhere to a four-tier classification system: [THEOREM] (analytic proof), [COMP_VERIF_CANDIDATE] (exhaustive computational verification, pending independent certification), [NUMERICAL] (empirical/heuristic observation), and [OPEN] (unsolved problem).

The candidate counterexample \(n^* = 896,315,812,331,399\) is classified as [COMP_VERIF_CANDIDATE, PENDING_INDEPENDENT_VERIFICATION]. It should not be regarded as a confirmed counterexample until an independent implementation (e.g., C++/SageMath) reproduces the result.

1. Background & Candidate Counterexample

In 2019, Zhi-Wei Sun conjectured that every positive integer \(n\) can be represented as:

\[ n = \binom{w}{2} + \binom{x}{4} + \binom{y}{6} + \binom{z}{8}, \qquad (w, x, y, z \ge 2) \]

Define the exceptional set \(E = \{n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1} : n \text{ has no representation}\}\). The strong form asserts \(E = \emptyset\), while the weak form asserts \(|E| < \infty\).

This paper presents an independent computational audit of the candidate counterexample \[ n^* = 896,315,812,331,399 \approx 8.963 \times 10^{14} \] (first identified by tadamcz in August 2026), which lies approximately 448.2 times beyond the previous verification bound of \(2 \times 10^{12}\).

Main Contributions & Results

  • [THEOREM] Search Domain Completeness: Proven individually sharp upper bounds for \(n^*\): \(z_{\max} = 281\), \(y_{\max} = 932\), \(x_{\max} = 12,112\), and \(w_{\max} = 42,339,481\).
  • [COMP_VERIF_CANDIDATE] Exhaustive Audit: Enumerated all 2,818,953,028 admissible \((z,y,x)\) triples with exact integer arithmetic. Zero representations were found in the complete domain.
  • [THEOREM] Modular Surjectivity (\(\mathcal{D}_3 \bmod 231 = \mathbb{Z}/231\mathbb{Z}\)): Proved via Cauchy-Davenport and CRT that \(n^* \bmod 231 = 152\) is modularly reachable, ruling out local residue obstructions modulo 231.
  • [NUMERICAL] Cantor-Pascal Diagnostics: Formulated ternary gap-order statistic \(fgo(n^*) = 9\) and identified a 4.6 order-of-magnitude scale mismatch between higher-order term steps and residual \(\rho\).
  • [OPEN] Research Agenda Redefinition: Shifted focus toward establishing whether the exceptional set \(E\) is finite or infinite, and whether \(n^* = \max(E)\).

Research Agenda & Open Problems

  • P0 [URGENT]: Independent verification of \(n^*\) via alternative programming environments (C++, SageMath).
  • P1 [CRITICAL]: Is the exceptional set \(E = \{n : n \text{ has no representation}\}\) finite or infinite?
  • P2 [HIGH]: Is \(n^* = 896,315,812,331,399\) the maximum element of \(E\)?
  • P3 [HIGH]: Prove the weak form: \(\exists N_0 \text{ s.t. } \forall n \ge N_0, n \notin E\).
  • P4 [HIGH]: Search for potential undiscovered exceptional elements in the interval \([2 \times 10^{12}, n^*)\).
  • P5 [MEDIUM]: Characterize the arithmetic structure and density of \(E\).
