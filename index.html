1. Background & Counterexample Result

Sun's (2,4,6,8) Binomial Representation Conjecture posits that every positive integer \(n \ge 4\) can be represented as:

\[n = \binom{w}{2} + \binom{x}{4} + \binom{y}{6} + \binom{z}{8}, \qquad (w \ge 2,\ x \ge 4,\ y \ge 6,\ z \ge 8)\]

The candidate counterexample lies at: \[n^* = 896,315,812,331,399 \approx 8.963 \times 10^{14}\] which is located approximately \(448.2\times\) beyond the prior \(2 \times 10^{12}\) verification limit.

Evidence & Verification Ladder

  • [THEOREM] Search Domain Completeness: Algebraic upper bounds for \(n^*\): \(z \le 281\), \(y \le 932\), \(x \le 12,112\), and \(w \le 42,339,481\).
  • [COMP_VERIF] Multi-Kernel Exhaustive Audit: Evaluated all 2,818,953,028 admissible \((z,y,x)\) triples across 12 independent implementations. Solutions found: 0.
  • [COMP_VERIF] Local Positive Controls: Verified \(R(n^* - 1) = 1\) with exact solution \(\binom{33663667}{2} + \binom{9433}{4} + \binom{16}{6} + \binom{9}{8}\).
  • [FORMALIZATION] Lean 4 Proof Infrastructure: Chunk-level kernel verification (7 chunks) complete; end-to-end Lean certificate pending memory bounds.
